# Writing for someone grieving

Nobody opens Lastly happy. Marie opens it a few weeks after her father died, and money is the last thing she wants to think about. Every word and every state on screen is written for her, on that day.

1. **Use his name.** Jean, or Jean Dupont. Never "the deceased", "the account holder" or "the estate" in what she reads. Legal terms belong in the letters and the printed report, where they are needed.
2. **Say what happens before the numbers.** "We will stop his StreamFlix and return the €9.99 it took after he died" comes before any table, ID or total.
3. **One thing at a time, and one approval where one is enough.** Ask her only what she alone can decide. If the rules already know the answer, don't make it a question.
4. **Say what she does not have to do.** "You don't have to call anyone. The letter is written; you only send it." Relief is information too.
5. **Say how long it takes, and that she can stop.** "It takes a few minutes. Nothing changes in PayPal until you approve, so you can stop at any point." Promise coming back later only where it is true.
6. **Never alarm.** Something waiting on a document, a merchant or a family member is *waiting*, not "blocked", "failed" or "error", and it is not red. Red is kept for the one thing that cannot be undone.
7. **Money plainly and once.** State an amount where it matters, in a normal sentence. No big celebratory totals, no "recovered", no success toasts, no exclamation marks.
8. **It is not a checkout.** No "Run", no "Release", no sales lines, no "Estate resolved". Calm verbs: stop, return, hand over, write, send.
9. **Honest when something is held.** If the documents disagree, say so plainly: what differs, that nothing has been touched, and what she can do next. Never pretend, never blame her.
10. **Respect without presuming feelings.** Not every family is close, and not every death is mourned the same way. Don't assume love for the person who died or goodwill between heirs: no "loved one", no "your family will be grateful", no letters written as if from one heir to another. Name people by their role ("the other heirs", "whoever keeps it") and state facts. A condolence said once and simply is enough.
11. **Detail one step back.** PayPal IDs, "Read by AI", the checks and the audit trail stay on the page for anyone who wants them (a judge, a notary), in smaller type, a "Details" toggle or the printed report. They are never the first thing she reads.

Never claim more than is true: no "bulletproof", no "can't be abused", no feature that does not exist.
